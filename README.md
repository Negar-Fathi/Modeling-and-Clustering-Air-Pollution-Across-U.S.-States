# Modeling-and-Clustering-Air-Pollution-Across-U.S.-States
Final Data Science Group Assignment - 2025

Air pollution is a pressing environmental and public health concern across the United States. Forecasting pollutant levels can help policymakers and citizens anticipate unhealthy conditions, plan mitigation strategies, and protect vulnerable populations. In this project, we explored a rich dataset of U.S. air quality measurements with the goal of deciding between two forecasting approaches: site-wise forecasting (predicting each monitoring station separately) versus overall daily trend forecasting (predicting the aggregated national or state-level daily averages). Through extensive exploratory data analysis (EDA), we uncovered evidence that site-wise forecasting suffers from instability, redundancy, and high noise, while aggregated daily forecasting provides a clearer and more reliable signal. We then tested a series of models ranging from baselines to advanced machine learning, validating that aggregated forecasting is both practical and effective.
